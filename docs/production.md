# Character and media production

Use this guide to move from a selected concept to an approved, repeatable character and its first media. Work in an installed studio. Higgsfield is the default media provider; the conversational assistant develops direction and uses verified tools. The [method guide](higgsfield-influencer-method.md) covers provider stages and [tools](tools.md) covers readiness.

## Define what must remain recognizable

Save an original adult character with an audience, personality, recurring premise and visual signature. Separate stable identity from variations:

| Layer | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Identity anchors | Facial structure, apparent age, proportions, distinctive marks and vocal signature |
| Style rules | Coherent ranges of expression, palette, editorial language, wardrobe and lighting |
| Content variations | Topic, setting, pose, emotion, camera and permitted styling changes |

Recognition should survive different clothes and lighting. Real-world credentials, product use, testimonials and results need evidence; fictional backstory is creative context.

## Explore and build references

When direction is open, compare distinct concepts before generation under [strategy](strategy.md). Direct expressive candidates and an in-character scene that demonstrate personality and the premise. Inspect results and let the user select the visual identity. Candidates stay draft material; never merge different candidates into one identity.

Develop coherent references from that exact selection. Choose the set required by the medium, for example:

- Front and three-quarter face views, simple lighting and neutral expression.
- Profile and smile for jaw, ears, nose and teeth.
- Half/full-body views for posture and proportions.
- A second expression/lighting condition and an in-character scene at the intended crop.
- A spoken reference sample when the character will speak.

A sheet or grid helps comparison, but the selected usable references need readable exact files. Inspect anatomy, recognition, presence and continuity across all chosen views. Record origin, framing, version, file/hash and limitations; preserve rejected attempts that help diagnosis.

For later generation/editing, attach actual selected references through supported inputs and record their roles/order and provider IDs. A filename, descriptive prompt or seed does not transmit a reference or prove fidelity. Check [reference transfer](higgsfield-plugin.md#transfer-exact-studio-references) before proposing manual attachment. Missing exact transport keeps generation pending.

## Complete visual and vocal canon

Initial visual selection is a checkpoint, not complete canon approval. For a new speaking character, generate a short vocal sample while it remains `draft`, with `purpose: reference`. Listen to the exact audio, check language/pronunciation/pacing and record the user's selection, settings and reference file/hash. Written voice direction alone is insufficient.

Once required visual and vocal references are reviewed and selected, record explicit complete canon approval, mark the persona `canon-approved` and create its snapshot through [operations](operations.md). Silent scope records why voice is not applicable. Reuse existing compatible approved voice and canon.

Voice settings and approved references contribute to the canon hash. A changed face/body/voice or other frozen anchor requires a new canon version and approval. New provider sheets remain derivative candidates; preserve historical bytes, snapshots and decisions.

## Prepare a piece

Give each piece an objective, audience, channel, format, message/payoff and intended action. Bind it to exact canon and narrative/content versions. Direct:

| Media | Useful brief details |
| --- | --- |
| Image | Setting, crop, perspective, light, pose, expression, wardrobe and required elements |
| Video | Exact speech or silent scope, shot sequence, subject/camera movement, gesture, pacing and audio |
| Delivery | Aspect ratio, resolution, duration, captions/text and channel crop constraints |

Translate the character's premise into a scene and performance. Generic quality adjectives do not replace direction. The local `prompt` command assembles a textual specification from persona/shot context; it does not attach files, route to a model or generate.

For video, prepare inspected scene inputs and save the [handoff package](production-handoff.md). Keep the production library distinct from the exact files each selected tool accepts.

## Produce one representative pilot

Check the complete capability/export/review path and bounded budget before paid work. Persist intent before submission and record actual tool/model, prompt/parameters, attached files/hashes, provider IDs, known costs and limitations. Reconcile uncertain jobs before another attempt.

After complete canon approval, make a small pilot in the intended medium. For speaking or performance content, use a short video with approved voice and relevant motion/lip-sync. An image-only deliverable uses an image pilot. Exercise the actual risks without requiring unnecessary shots.

Save real output bytes as new versions, register them and create the applicable `execution-seal`. Inspect against exact canon and script using [quality](quality.md). Batches require complete review of the pilot with `approve`, no blocking issues and no required media left uninspected. Correct failures in new versions and repeat review/approval; materially changed production conditions need a suitable new pilot.

## Review, deliver and learn

Review final exports again after editing, captions, music, cropping or compression. Record exact files/hashes, actual reviewer/method, decision and remaining limitations. Missing listening, motion access or export keeps the corresponding stage pending. A completed job, seal or local test does not approve an asset.

Deliver the reviewed bytes. Publication requires applicable authorization and actual post evidence. Compare real results with suitable windows, exposure and costs under [strategy](strategy.md); a portrait or attractive pilot alone does not establish viral performance. Back up important character/media/run records through local operations.

For multiple characters, keep references, voices, scripts, records and decisions separate. Shared campaign styling can reuse generic resources; each identity and its interactions still need their own review.
