# OLYMPOX influencer production

This workflow prepares original, memorable, repeatable characters before producing campaigns. ChatGPT/Codex develops concept, personality, narrative world, scripts and planning. **Integrated ChatGPT/Codex image generation** is the default for appearance, candidates, references, settings, images and edits when available, with no Higgsfield, Builder, external CLI or API key required. Respect the user's explicit choice of another method or provider. Choose verified voice, animation, video, lip-sync and specialized tools per stage according to need, quality and known cost; Higgsfield remains optional. Every route retains complete review, exact canon approval, an inspected pilot, authorized publication and measured comparison. Integrated generation depends on account limits and tool availability, and is neither promised free nor unlimited.

## Record the method and stages

Start with the adaptive conversation and concept cards in [strategy](strategy.md). Save a versioned [production-method plan](../templates/production-method.md) in the installed studio and track the actual file as a run input or planning output through [core operation](framework-02.md). State each stage's objective, selected concept, method, tool, exposed model, provider/module when applicable, access route, source evidence, prompts, exact inputs/references with hashes, dependencies, outputs, known costs, authorizations and limitations. Record unavailable model or cost information as unknown instead of inventing it. The plan uses existing file/hash and attempt handling; it is not a provider adapter or new semantic runtime enforcement.

Check the actual integrated generation and inspection tools before visual work; OLYMPOX's local commands prepare records and specifications, and do not call a model. If a required capability is unavailable, prepare independent local work, record that stage as pending or `awaiting-tool`, and propose alternatives. A switch to paid external generation requires applicable authorization. When Higgsfield is useful or explicitly selected for a stage, follow the [optional Higgsfield method](higgsfield-influencer-method.md) and verify that operation separately. Builder is not a prerequisite for integrated visuals or other downstream tools unless the user's selected process explicitly requires it. A changed tracked method follows the existing new-attempt procedure; preserve uncertain external jobs for reconciliation before resubmitting.

For existing characters, reuse their approved canon, unchanged approved voice and valid prior stage evidence without repeating approval. Do not migrate them automatically to the new visual default, restart identity creation or force a new Builder identity. When changing a recorded production route, use the exact approved references and inspect every new output against them; it remains a candidate until reviewed. Missing attachment/import or fidelity leaves that stage pending. Adding or changing voice in frozen canon follows normal identity-version evolution. Historical characters, media, approvals, snapshots, backups and runs retain their original bytes and evidence.

## 1. Define the character

Before generating media, resolve the proposition: audience, topic, memorable premise, personality, values, language, speaking style, editorial contrast and reason to follow this influencer. When direction is open, present at least three distinct realistic concepts with strong presence and concrete hooks before portraits, unless another visual language was selected, following [strategy](strategy.md). The user selects the direction; save the concept before defining the final name and identity. Establish explicit adult age and create an original identity. Visual references guide aesthetics; using a real person's face or voice requires appropriate authorization.

Separate three layers:

| Layer | Definition | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity anchors | Traits that must remain recognizable. | Facial structure, body proportions, apparent age, distinctive marks, and vocal signature. |
| Style rules | Coherent ranges of expression and presentation. | Palette, language, energy, makeup, wardrobe, and lighting treatment. |
| Content variations | Elements that can change per piece. | Setting, permitted hairstyle, clothing, pose, topic, camera, and emotion. |

Variable details must not become the only way to recognize the person. A character needs to remain identifiable in different clothes and lighting.

## 2. Build the reference pack

For a new influencer, generate an exploration sample with the available integrated image tool, or the user's selected route. Direct expression, posture, styling, framing and a scene that expresses the selected personality and premise. Inspect concept and identity, obtain the user's visual selection and adjust from feedback before expanding the reference set. Develop coherent views from the exact selected candidate instead of inventing a new face for each angle. Do not randomize an existing approved identity or treat a preset name as the user's identity choice. Candidate media remains `draft` and reference records remain `candidate` until reviewed. Do not assemble images of different candidates as though they were a single identity.

The initial pack should cover:

- Front and three-quarter face views from both sides, with a neutral expression and simple lighting.
- Profile and smile, to check the nose, jaw, ears, and teeth.
- Half-body and full-body views, with natural posture and clothing that allows assessment of proportions.
- A different lighting condition and expression, to test recognition beyond the main portrait.
- An in-character scene or cover that demonstrates the selected premise, distinctive presence and attitude at the intended crop.
- If voice is included: an approved spoken sample with a neutral sentence, a question, and moderate emotion, accompanied by the language, accent, rhythm, and timbre description.

These framings are an initial proposal, not a quality guarantee. Use only the set needed for the character and production medium. Invisible or uninspected material cannot be an approved reference.

A reference sheet can display multiple views, but a grid alone does not establish readable, separate references for each angle. Obtain the required exact files through a verified export or extraction route, preserve the source sheet when one exists, and inspect every selected reference. Use the integrated route for reference and scene development by default; selected specialized tools still need actual capability and input checks. Soul ID or other identity training is optional, justified by a demonstrated need, and separately authorized.

For each subsequent generation or edit, inspect the selected reference files and attach them through the image tool's supported reference input, preserving their exact paths/identifiers and hashes in provenance. Use local image paths when the tool accepts them; otherwise use the smallest supported set of actual conversation attachments that contains the required references. If the tool cannot receive all required references, retain the stage pending and request the missing attachment or propose a verified transfer route. Merely naming a file, describing its face, copying a seed or retaining conversation text does not prove reference conditioning. Inspect recognition, anatomy and continuity across the actual resulting angles and scenes.

Each reference needs an exact file, framing description, origin, and observations. Choose a main reference and coherent complementary references. Preserve original files and the pack version. Naming an image "official" does not approve it: review it using the [quality guide](quality.md) and record the initial visual selection while the new persona remains `draft`.

If the character will speak, generate a short scripted vocal sample in draft/reference exploration using `purpose: reference` and a verified voice tool selected for the stage. Listen to the exact audio and record the user's selection, exact file/hash, settings and approved reference before final canon approval. Written voice descriptions are not audio references. If generation or listening is unavailable, vocal selection and complete canon approval remain pending. For a silent persona, record voice as not applicable to the selected scope. Only after the required visual and vocal selections are complete should the responsible person approve the full canon, mark the persona `canon-approved` and create its preserved snapshot through [operations](operations.md). Voice settings and approved references contribute to the canon hash; adding a voice after freezing the same version is a canon change.

## 3. Test consistency before batches

Scripts can be prepared during draft/reference exploration, with concrete openings, fulfilled payoffs and the character's recognizable perspective. At production, preserve the exact spoken text and use the already selected, listened-to vocal reference bound to approved canon. Do not add a new voice to a frozen version to satisfy the pilot.

After complete visual/vocal canon approval, make a small production pilot using the chosen tools for its actual media type, covering real content situations: close-up, smile, half-body, full-body, and a different setting. An image-only pilot can use integrated generation throughout; video uses verified capabilities for the required movement, speech and lip-sync. For video, include short speech with the approved voice, a moderate head turn, and a simple gesture when they serve the planned content; a silent persona's pilot uses no speech. Prepare production scenes/stills before the video calls that depend on them. If the first campaign does not use some of these elements, adapt the pilot to actual needs and record the reason.

Compare every result with canon without adding failures to the pack itself. If the method works only at one angle or depends on hiding the face, resolve that before promising recurring production. Approve individual assets; do not approve the whole method based on a single good take.

## 4. Write a brief for each piece

Every brief needs an objective, channel, audience, format, main message, desired action, and identity reference. Include what actually guides production:

- **Photography:** setting, framing, lens or desired perspective, light direction, pose, gaze, expression, wardrobe, and mandatory elements.
- **Video:** spoken script, target duration, shot sequence, movements, gestures, pacing, voice, and audio composition. Short sentences and one main movement per take simplify diagnosis and editing.
- **Delivery:** intended aspect ratio, crop, resolution, and duration; caption or text; placement of elements that need to survive channel cropping.
- **Constraints:** anchors to preserve, authorized variations, and required rights.
- **Creative promise:** the chosen concept, attitude/editorial perspective, opening, payoff and reason to watch/save/share; translate these into the scene, framing, styling and performance.

Naturalism needs direction: textured skin, plausible hair, coherent light, and human gestures. Expressions such as "perfect," "hyper-beautiful," or "8K" do not replace these decisions and can push the result toward an artificial appearance.

The local `prompt` command includes nonempty audience, value proposition, personality and fictional backstory as creative context alongside identity and shot details. Review that specification against the current concept; fictional background does not establish real credentials or experiences. The command prepares text only and neither routes to Builder nor submits references. A neutral portrait specification tests identity; use separate scene specifications to express the character's presence and creative promise.

## 5. Produce and record

For planned video, follow [From Codex preparation to a video pilot](production-handoff.md) and save the [video handoff package](../templates/video-handoff.md). Prepare the approved character, voice when needed, scripts and inspected scene images before mapping each shot to a verified specialized operation. The local library and actual accepted submission inputs are recorded separately; a pilot validates the handoff before batches.

Use integrated image generation for visual work by default when available. Honor an explicit selected route and select specialized tools per stage instead of applying one provider to the whole pipeline. Optional Higgsfield access can use its [Codex plugin](higgsfield-plugin.md) or the [local CLI and wrapper](higgsfield-setup.md), only when that route exposes the operation required by the plan. The plugin route requires no local Higgsfield CLI; check its actual tools, account access, reference inputs, costs, and output retrieval separately. Plugin availability does not establish Builder, voice, Soul ID, export, or equivalent CLI billing. Every route follows the same canon, authorization, records and review requirements.

Use the selected candidate during draft reference development and approved exact references for production. Record each stage's method, tool/model when exposed, canon version, prompt, relevant parameters, attached references, input/output files and hashes, known cost and limitations. Record a seed only if the tool exposes one; it helps traceability but does not guarantee the same identity across tools, versions, or settings. Account limits and unavailable cost/model information remain explicit; integrated generation is not described as free or unlimited.

Persist submission intent in the run before calling an external tool, then record returned job IDs, status, known cost, and retrievable output files. Use actual tool evidence for the selected route; a plugin call does not create a local OLYMPOX record automatically. Query and reconcile uncertain results before another submission. Preserve the exact generation context through the execution seal and bind review to the final files and hashes. Missing output retrieval or inspection keeps the corresponding stage pending.

A local Windows canon path does not establish that any tool can read or attach it. Verify the supported attachment/upload route and record the references actually submitted. Save actual generated bytes locally, hash them and inspect them before registering an approved reference or delivery. A remote URL or gallery remains a provider result until the final bytes are saved locally and inspected; follow the [plugin guide](higgsfield-plugin.md) when Higgsfield is selected for this handoff.

Generate a candidate, review, and adjust. When testing a hypothesis, change one main variable at a time. Produce batches only after finding a configuration that works in the pilot. Define an attempt and cost limit for each piece before using paid services; if the limit is reached, stop and review the brief or method.

Keep source files, working versions, and exports separate and identifiable. Also retain rejected attempts when they help diagnose a failure; never mix rejected results into the approved pack.

## 6. Approve and export

Follow the [quality guide](quality.md), including temporal review and listening for videos. A local correction does not waive review of the whole result. Review the final export again after editing, compression, music, captions, or cropping.

Moving to `production` requires an inspected final file and a recorded decision. Preparing a file for publication is a separate stage from publishing: the state does not authorize posting, scheduling, or sending to third parties.

## Operating multiple characters

Keep identity, pack, briefs, scripts, versions, and reviews separate for each character. Each task must declare the character and canon version before selecting references. Share only generic resources such as export presets and brief templates; do not reuse another influencer's faces, voices, or distinctive marks without checking.

A campaign can have shared style without unintentionally bringing identities closer together. When two people appear in one shot, review each and their interaction: scale, contact, gaze direction, and continuity.

## Recommended first cycle

1. Discover only unresolved preferences; compare three concepts when direction is open and save the selected concept and production-method plan.
2. For a new influencer, use integrated image generation by default for expressive exploration and a premise scene; record the user's visual selection, then attach the exact selected references to coherent reference views and subsequent images/edits.
3. Keep the new persona `draft` after initial visual selection. Prepare scripts and, if speaking, a draft/reference voice sample; listen and record its selected file/settings/reference. Then obtain final visual/vocal canon approval and freeze the snapshot. Record voice as not applicable for a silent persona; reuse unchanged approved voice for an existing character.
4. Produce a small pilot with the verified tools selected per stage; correct actual observed problems, inspect images and complete motion/audio when present, review the final export, and approve individual assets before batches.
5. Publish only when authorized, record actual post IDs and dates, and compare equivalent windows, formats, exposure, sharing/retention and cost under [strategy](strategy.md). Without publication or metrics, retain the measurement stage as pending.
6. Save learning and choose the next experiment from actual results. Do not declare the method viral from an attractive portrait or a completed job.

This cycle makes clear what was approved, what still needs inspection, and which production conditions were actually exercised. Expand the process according to verified needs without turning hypotheses into quality guarantees.
